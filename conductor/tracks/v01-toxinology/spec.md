# V01 — specification

## Clinical question and boundary

What additional clinically useful information can be recovered from existing envenomation records and publications, and which differences in presentation, ascertainment, exposure and outcome are supported by independent evidence? Prioritise public-data secondary research, particularly Irukandji syndrome, within the Toxinology stream. Use animal taxonomy, documented ecology, developmental biology and published molecular observations only where they inform human exposure, diagnosis, prevention or clinical heterogeneity. Distinguish animal biological variation, exposure, human host variation and healthcare/reporting effects. Species-level evidence is not the exposure of an individual patient; neither geographic proxies nor molecular annotations establish patient identity, dose or causal clinical effects. No toxin optimisation, new perturbation experiments, or unrelated animal-genomics programme.

## Incremental information, not repeated publication

Create a study–report–dataset–event/assay crosswalk. Distinguish confirmed overlap, possible overlap, independent sources and unknowns. Record original questions, endpoints, models, publication tables, time/region, source restrictions and available unused observations. Reproduce material historical comparisons as QC or justified independent replication; then assess new subgroups, source contrasts, missingness/ascertainment, clinical variables, temporal information, complementary data and previously unreported relationships. Freeze primary estimands and analysis families at N1, record all prespecified estimates, uncertainty, null findings, deviations and failures; exploratory findings remain labelled and require validation. Do not select statistically significant or apparently novel outcomes alone. Publication foregrounds distinct contribution without suppressing relevant negative results.

## Clinical source questions

1. What information is newly available in the expanded public extract versus the first release and published clinical cohorts? Count distinct records, unique events where identifiable, specimens, sources and missingness separately.
2. How do reporting source, first care contact, subsequent transport/ED attendance and actual clinical phenotype differ? Hospital, lifesaving, media and research records are not mutually exclusive patient cohorts. Compare common time/geography and source-specific completeness; establish referral/ascertainment bias, uncertainty and denominator limits. No risk/incidence or PPE efficacy claim from case-only counts.
3. What does narrative extraction add beyond the structured fields, and do independent annotations support improved accuracy or coverage? Candidate fields: symptom presence/absence, time course, anatomical site, age, care setting, intervention, disposition, uncertainty and documented species identification. Retain exact source field/span, assertion status, time relation, subject, original literal, mapping/version, confidence and abstention. Do not infer missing clinical information; source documentation is not clinical ground truth.
4. Where species/life-stage identification is directly documented, do phenotype patterns differ? Separate confirmed identification, suspected/ambiguous and unknown; compare clinical context, geography, age and ascertainment only where design permits. Document within-species heterogeneity and ecological confounding. Molecular and published functional evidence provides context, not an individual causal attribution or experimental design to increase harmful effects.
5. What existing public human host-response datasets can add independently? GSE126575 is a cell-line perturbation benchmark, not Irukandji patient data. Any clinical extrapolation needs separate validation; T06 remains conditional.

## Method and data gates

Follow existing N0–N3, source access/ethics, per-artifact rights, biological/event independence, method qualification and official-device policy. Compare structured-only and deterministic rules/dictionary extraction before any existing clinical NLP/LLM route. Use a stratified double-annotated, adjudicated reference sample and a locked event/source-separated evaluation set; freeze label definitions, sampling, metrics and clinically justified error thresholds before evaluation. Measure precision/recall, F1 and confidence intervals by field/source, false assertions, negation, temporality, attribution, missingness/abstention, consistency and review effort. Audit disagreements and estimate extraction-error sensitivity in downstream summaries. Computational methods may be reproducible but are not inherently more accurate than humans. Use local or specifically approved processing only; no public patient narratives, model uploads or patient-level cloud execution. A tool benchmark is not clinical admission. Follow CINEX (https://doi.org/10.1093/jamia/ocag136), STROBE/RECORD where applicable, and the existing reporting-standard approach.

## Exit criteria and stops

Actual source-byte receipts, permissions and release differences; quantified field and source completeness, duplicate/overlap assessment, identified usable clinical unit; documented prior-art delta and a defensible estimand; validated extraction only if it adds information or efficiency at acceptable error; explicit admission or hold/stop decision with owners. Publication requires N1 then N2 and N3. No claim of source-group comparisons, clinical association, data linkage or novel finding merely from metadata. If narrative or source labels are absent, fall back to a narrower structured-data study or stop. Source-dependent restrictions or unidentifiable estimands block only affected claims.

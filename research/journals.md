> **Current submission preference, 22 November 2026:** Target clinically oriented, moderate-scope journals before higher-impact outlets. **European Journal of Human Genetics, Genetics in Medicine and Clinical Epigenetics are not routine targets** for this programme, regardless of previous publication history. Journal fit does not prove higher acceptance rates. Execute [N0/N1 before and N2 after each analysis](novelty-priority-protocol.md), and N3 before submission; evaluate article type, indexing, ethics requirements, clinical contribution, fees and related-publication overlap. *Molecular Syndromology* currently offers full research articles and brief reports and notes a change in hosting from Karger to Oxford Academic in February 2027 (https://academic.oup.com/msy/pages/author-guidelines). Wiley currently reports a 9% acceptance rate for *Molecular Genetics & Genomic Medicine* (https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/journal/23249269), so lower impact must **not** be equated with an easier submission.

# Clinical-first journal routing (provisional)

Scope fit is not a measured probability of acceptance. **Do not describe a title as easy to publish in** merely because its impact factor is lower. Verify current article types, indexing, fees, transfers, and editorial policy when the manuscript actually exists.

**Helpful 2026 publisher disclosures:** Wiley currently lists about **29%** acceptance for AJMG Part A versus about **9%** for Molecular Genetics & Genomic Medicine. They are not adjusted for article mix or comparable submission populations, but they caution against assuming a lower-impact broadly scoped journal is easier. Check the rates at submission. Source pages: https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/journal/15524833 and https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/journal/23249269.

**Specific caution:** Clinical Epigenetics has published requirements for independent biological validation, sufficient power, raw-data access and functional interpretation of reused datasets; not the initial low-friction target. Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11682640/.

**Process:** select 1 primary + 1 backup at the start of a study, but lock journal only after results; avoid redundant salami publications. Clinical usefulness beats complexity and journal prestige.

**Preferred submission sequence:** Kabuki replication to *Molecular Syndromology* or *European Journal of Medical Genetics*; cross-disease study to *European Journal of Medical Genetics* or *American Journal of Medical Genetics Part A* where phenotypes matter; atlas/methods to *BMC Medical Genomics* only when it answers a clinical question; toxin modelling to *Toxicon: X* or *Toxicon*. Higher-profile genetics journals are optional only on an explicit later request or exceptionally strong clinical evidence. No manuscript should be submitted if results only repeat existing studies or lack appropriate ethics/rights documentation.
